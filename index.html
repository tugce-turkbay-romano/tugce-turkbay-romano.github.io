01 · Projects & evidence

Two complementary assessment methods

Disassemblability and repairability are treated as related but distinct engineering capabilities, each connected to measurable design requirements and operational decisions.

Disassemblability assessment

Disassemblability Index for Power Electronic Converters

Problem
Existing disassembly metrics do not adequately capture PCB-level operations, including component removal and desoldering.
Method
Experimental disassembly analysis and adaptation of the ease of Disassembly Metric (eDiM) to PCB-based power electronic converters.
Evidence
Application to a power electronic converter, including component-level disassembly sequences and connection technologies.
Contribution
A PCB-based disassemblability assessment method with quantifiable requirements for circular product design.
Decision relevance
Supports connection-technology selection, product redesign, repair, refurbishment and remanufacturing decisions.

The method adapts eDiM to PCB-level operations so designers can formulate measurable disassemblability requirements.

Repairability assessment

Repairability Index for PCB Electronic Circuits

Problem
Aggregated repairability scores can conceal critical repair barriers and commonly treat the PCB as a replaceable unit rather than a repairable system.
Method
Repair-workflow modelling, analysis of existing indices, interviews with eight repair experts and a weighting survey involving 50 electronics and eco-design experts.
Evidence
Five weighted assessment categories, two PCB case studies and an open-access Excel assessment tool.
Contribution
A technically grounded PCB repairability index with warning indicators that prevent critical barriers from being hidden by the overall score.
Decision relevance
Helps repair professionals evaluate feasibility and identify bottlenecks while providing actionable eco-design feedback to manufacturers.

Five criteria—traceability, design features, disassembly/reassembly, diagnostics and spare parts—are complemented by warning indicators for critical barriers.

Circular modularity

Towards Circular Power Electronics Through Modularity

Problem
Modularity in power electronic converters is often driven by electrical topology and performance rather than by structural requirements for repair, reuse, upgrade and recycling.
Method
Review and conceptual analysis of modularity in power electronics, examining four converter architectures—PEBB, MIC, MMC and PCA—and their functional and structural modularity.
Evidence
Comparison of existing PEC modular architectures and design recommendations for four end-of-life strategies: repair and maintenance, reuse, upgrade and recycling.
Contribution
A circular-design perspective for defining PEC modules according to component reliability, lifetime, value and material compatibility, together with requirements for detachable and standardized interfaces.
Decision relevance
Supports early architecture decisions on module definition, component grouping and interconnection design while making trade-offs between detachability, electrical efficiency, cost and reliability explicit.

Circular modularity requires more than modular circuit topology: modules should also be structurally separable and designed around their intended end-of-life strategy.
