01 · Current research agenda

From product assessment to organizational performance

A research programme connecting product characteristics, organizational controls, circular practices and sustainability outcomes.

01

Integrating product indicators into management control

How can repairability, disassemblability, durability and residual-value indicators be incorporated into planning, investment decisions and performance-control systems?

02

Evaluating circular strategies

Under what technical, organizational and market conditions do repair, reuse, refurbishment and remanufacturing improve environmental and economic performance?

03

Governing circular product information

How can digital product information support coordination among designers, manufacturers, repairers, recyclers and decision-makers while remaining reliable, comparable and operationally useful?

Methods

Engineering assessment · multicriteria decision analysis · material and information-flow modelling · life-cycle assessment · case studies · design-science research

Expected contribution

An empirically validated framework connecting product characteristics, organizational controls, circular practices and sustainability outcomes.

Research mechanism

Evidence is useful only when it informs a decision.

Data collected

Product architecture, component accessibility, disassembly operations, technical condition, functional performance, lifetime, market value and lifecycle information.

Indicators constructed

Engineering variables are structured through component-level assessment, multicriteria modelling and lifecycle methods, then tested for sensitivity, interpretability and decision usefulness.

Decisions informed

Repair, redesign, reuse, refurbishment, remanufacturing, investment and end-of-use strategy selection at product and organizational levels.

Effects measured

Environmental and economic outcomes are evaluated as research questions—not assumed benefits—alongside implementation feasibility and organizational performance.

Contribution to sustainability management

Connecting technical circularity with organizational implementation.

My research extends engineering-based circularity assessment toward organizational implementation. It examines how product and lifecycle information can become part of management-control systems, investment decisions and sustainability-performance measurement. The objective is to identify not only whether a product is technically circular, but also whether organizations can implement, monitor and derive value from circular strategies.

03 · Projects & evidence

Two complementary assessment methods

Disassemblability and repairability are treated as related but distinct engineering capabilities, each connected to measurable design requirements and operational decisions.

Disassemblability assessment

Disassemblability of Power Electronic Converters

Problem
Existing disassembly metrics do not adequately capture PCB-level operations, including component removal and desoldering.
Method
Experimental disassembly analysis and adaptation of the ease of Disassembly Metric (eDiM) to PCB-based power electronic converters.
Evidence
Application to a power electronic converter, including component-level disassembly sequences and connection technologies.
Contribution
A PCB-based disassemblability assessment method with quantifiable requirements for circular product design.
Decision relevance
Supports connection-technology selection, product redesign, repair, refurbishment and remanufacturing decisions.

The method adapts eDiM to PCB-level operations so designers can formulate measurable disassemblability requirements.

Repairability assessment

Repairability Index for PCB Electronic Circuits

Problem
Aggregated repairability scores can conceal critical repair barriers and commonly treat the PCB as a replaceable unit rather than a repairable system.
Method
Repair-workflow modelling, analysis of existing indices, interviews with eight repair experts and a weighting survey involving 50 electronics and eco-design experts.
Evidence
Five weighted assessment categories, two PCB case studies and an open-access Excel assessment tool.
Contribution
A technically grounded PCB repairability index with warning indicators that prevent critical barriers from being hidden by the overall score.
Decision relevance
Helps repair professionals evaluate feasibility and identify bottlenecks while providing actionable eco-design feedback to manufacturers.

Five criteria—traceability, design features, disassembly/reassembly, diagnostics and spare parts—are complemented by warning indicators for critical barriers.
